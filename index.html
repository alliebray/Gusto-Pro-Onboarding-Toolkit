Pipeline math — know this cold
📊 55% rule
Have 55% of next month's adds target built before that month starts.

Example: Sept target = 20 adds → have 11 adds in pipeline before Aug 31
📅 The 15th rule
By the 15th, open funnel + closed won adds = 100% of monthly target. If you're there, switch immediately to building for next month.

Example: Sept target = 20 adds → CW + open opps ≥ 20 adds by Sept 15
⏱ Time split
70% of your time on top-of-funnel creation.
30% on conversion.

Don't just chase opps — be an advisor to your partners.
Tip: No matter where you're pacing for the month, always switch to building for next month on the 15th. Always.

Discovery & Command of the Message

CoM is not a script — it's a way of thinking. Listen first, connect what you heard to a specific Gusto outcome, then prescribe. The TED-W questions below are your tools to get there.

Command of the Message — the framework
Core idea: You are a disruptive sale. You're asking partners and clients to change behavior — stop acting one way and start acting another. Your job is to teach for differentiation, not to pitch features.
1. Teach for differentiation
Engage with a unique perspective about their business. Deliver insight they didn't have before the call — not a product demo.
2. Tailor for resonance
Know their economic and value drivers before you propose anything. What does success look like for their firm — more clients, more advisory revenue, less admin time?
3. Take control
Be comfortable discussing money. Press the customer — not aggressively, but confidently. A great rep can "pressure" the customer toward a decision with care.
4. Two-way communication
Strong discovery is a dialogue. Ask, listen, reflect back. Don't present until you've earned the right to be heard.
The CoM bridge — connecting discovery to your pitch
1
Reflect back what you heard
"What I heard you say was [X] — does that sound right / is it fair to say that?"
2
Connect to a specific outcome
"Because of that, I recommend…" / "Here's how we solve for that."
3
Prescribe with confidence
"Based on what I heard, here's what I recommend…" — then be specific. Not vague. Name the plan, the feature, the next step.
✓
Create urgency through clarity, not pressure
"Since you mentioned wanting to [goal] before [timeframe], let's review which clients are the best fit. Does Tuesday or Wednesday work for that?"
TED-W discovery questions — firm-specific
TED-W stands for: Tell me more  ·  Explain  ·  Describe  ·  What / Why / When / How. These are open-ended probes that keep the partner talking and reveal what they actually care about — which is rarely what they say upfront.
T — Tell me more
"Tell me more about your mission and why you started this firm."
"Tell me more about how your clients typically come to you."
"Tell me more about what's happened with those clients that switched providers."
"Tell me more about your billing model and how you structure firm vs. client billing."
E — Explain
"Explain how you qualify a new client before bringing them onto Gusto."
"Explain what your typical client onboarding process looks like from start to first payroll."
"Explain how you're handling payroll for clients who are in multiple states."
"Explain what advisory means to your firm — are you doing it today or is it aspirational?"
D — Describe
"Describe what a perfect quarter looks like for your firm."
"Describe your current tech stack — what tools are you using to run payroll, track time, and manage clients?"
"Describe how you're currently getting paid by your clients — invoicing, auto-billing, rev share?"
"Describe what's clunky about your current client intake process."
W — What / Why / When / How
"What are the top 3 goals for your firm this year?"
"Why have you chosen [ADP / Paychex / QBO] for those specific clients?"
"When a client gives you negative feedback — what does that typically sound like?"
"How are you helping clients understand and actually act on their financial reports?"
"What would make this partnership a 10 out of 10 for you?"

Escalation paths

Self-serve first, then escalate with full context. Know which path before the situation is live.

Golden rule: Escalate early — a 24-hour heads-up beats a last-minute surprise. Come with context (deal summary, close date, revenue impact), not just a problem.
SituationSelf-serve firstThen escalate toSLA
Deal / Pricing
Discount beyond matrix, non-standard terms, multi-year		 Run CPQ scenario, check discount matrix, review past precedents Post to Deal Desk directly → VP Sales if above threshold → Finance for large ARR Same day (in-quarter)
Support unresolved
Ticket >48hrs, customer escalation, bug impacting workflow		 Check ticket status, review account health score, check if Gold tier (→ DSA) Chatter case owner in SF PE → no response 24hr: tag owner's manager → #gpro-sales-escalation-station if no case owner 24hr internal
Technical / Product bug
Bug blocking a deal, API failure, feature gap		 Check #all-product-feedback, search known issues log, check product status page Submit SF ticket to Solutions Eng → Slack SE directly → Jira TPO ticket if confirmed bug P1: 2hr · P2: 1 day
Legal / Contract
MSA, DPA, BAA, redlines, IP pushback		 Check standard redline library, review pre-approved fallback terms, create PLO in SFDC PLO → manager review → routes to Legal based on contract type 3–5 business days
Risk / NACHA
Expedite approval, manual signatory, NACHA errors		 Review risk approval matrix, confirm payment terms and account info Submit SF risk ticket → flag manager if urgent → Risk team reviews 24hr standard
SE involvement
Complex technical, custom integration, security deep-dive		 Review SE processes in Slack, submit SFDC ticket for SE SE assigned via SF ticket → SE joins next customer call. AE drives, SE owns technical scope. SE assigned within 1 day
